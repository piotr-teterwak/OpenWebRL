# Iterative on-policy DPO (Tillicum, 2026-10-19 to 10-30)

Can a second round of ARM-guided on-policy DPO improve on the first? Short answer: **no**. Round 1 is a solid
gain over the SFT actor; none of nine round-2 variants beats round 1. The first round-2 attempt failed through
length drift; fixing the drift (reference = base, combined data) removes the cut-offs but does not add success.

Round-2 tables are regenerated by `tillicum/iterative_tables.py`; round-1 sweep numbers are from
`tillicum/EXPERIMENT_LOG.md` (§2). *Overall* = successes / all tasks (invalid = failure); *cut-off* = episodes
ended by the 1,024-token per-generation cap.

## Setup

- **Actor**: OpenWebRL-4B-SFT (Qwen3-VL-4B). **Selector (ARM)**: SelectionARM (`PTeterwak/OpenWebRL-4B-SelectionARM`
  @ 81b452d). Collection: each step the actor samples 5 candidates (T 0.7, top-p 0.9), the ARM picks one.
- **Pairs**: chosen = the ARM's executed pick in a *successful* episode; rejected = another candidate at the same
  state; ambiguous pairs dropped; **equal tool-call counts required** (without it DPO learns "more actions wins"
  and ~80% of episodes hit the token cap). Builder: `scripts/prepare_arm_joint_data.py`.
- **Training**: DPO on a LoRA, `scripts/train_arm_joint_ddp.py` via `tillicum/train_joint.sbatch`; β 0.3, lr 5e-5,
  one epoch, summed full-response log-probs, gated on generation length. Reference = the start model with its
  adapters disabled unless stated.
- **Eval**: `openwebrl.arm_eval`, one actor sample per step. Every round-2 model was evaluated on
  Online-Mind2Web (300 tasks, gpt-5.6-terra judge) **in the same job as the round-1 model**, so each is compared
  with round 1 in its own pass; the round-1 model itself ranges 29.3–37.0% overall across 21 passes (mean 34.1),
  so single-pass differences under ~4 points are noise.

## Round 2: nine variants

Round-2 collections used the round-1 model (`onpol-dpo-b0.3-s1`) as the actor, with the ARM picking, on 2,091
tasks: the **same C2 pool** (1,150 successful tasks, 9,058 turns → 2,752 pairs) or a **new pool** of unseen tasks
on the same websites and difficulty (`scripts/build_matched_task_pool.py`; 1,102 successful tasks, 9,324 turns).
Every run starts from the round-1 model except the combined-data run, which starts from SFT.

| Variant | Pairs | Seeds 1 / 2 / 42, overall − round 1 | Mean | Cut-off (round 1: 3–7%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Plain round 2 (reference = round 1) | 2,752 | −1.0 / −2.3 / −6.3 | **−3.2** | **16%** |
| B. A + SFT term (RPO, weight 1) | 2,752 | 0.0 / +3.0 / −0.3 | +0.9 | 13% |
| C. Round-1 + round-2 pairs, one DPO pass from SFT | 7,958 | 0.0 / −1.7 / +1.3 | −0.1 | 1% |
| D. A with reference = SFT base | 2,752 | −0.7 / −0.3 / −6.3 | −2.4 | 3% |
| E. Luna (gpt-6-luna) re-picks the chosen action (reference = round 1) | 2,786 | −4.3 / +5.7 / −0.3 | +0.3 | 7% |
| F. Only pairs where Luna agrees with the ARM (reference = base) | 1,526 | −2.7 / +3.7 / −1.3 | −0.1 | 3% |
| G. New task pool; rejected ≥ 0.3 below chosen in ARM probability (ref = base) | 2,490 | +0.7 / −1.3 / −1.7 | −0.8 | 4% |
| H. G with Luna labels at every state | 2,468 | excluded / −1.7 / −1.0 | −1.3 | 2% |
| I. G keeping only states where Luna picks the executed action | 1,292 | −3.7 / −3.0 / −1.7 | −2.8 | 4% |

Seed 1 of H is excluded: that pass was an infrastructure failure (64 and 54 of 300 valid for H and round 1).
Per-seed paired intervals are in the script output; only two are significant: A seed 42 (−6.8 [−12.8, −1.2]) and
D seed 42 (−8.3 [−13.9, −2.4]) below round 1, and E seed 2 (+6.3 [+0.4, +12.3]) above it.

### What the variants show

1. **Plain iteration hurts through length drift** (A). Round-2 episodes hit the token cap 14–19% of the time
   against ~4% for round 1; round-2 chosen responses are 17% longer. The round-1 actor's longer outputs are what
   the ARM then picks in successful episodes, and DPO pushes further in that direction.
2. **Fixing the drift does not recover a gain.** Reference = base (D) and combined data (C) bring cut-offs to
   1–3%, at or below round 1, yet D is −2.4 and C −0.1. The episodes that no longer truncate end by exhausting the
   step limit instead (D: 53 vs 45 step-limit episodes in seed 1).
3. **Better labels do not help.** Re-picking with a stronger selector (E), filtering to agreement (F), or both on
   fresh tasks (H, I) are all within noise of round 1; I (fewest pairs, 1,292) is the weakest.
4. **Fresh tasks do not help.** The new pool with a probability margin (G) is −0.8; the round-2 data is not
   limited by task overlap with round 1.
5. **RPO is the least bad** (B, +0.9) but still has 13% cut-offs; its gain is one seed.

## Conclusion

One round of ARM-guided on-policy DPO captures the available gain (+9 OM2W, +5 to +10 WebVoyager). A second round
from the round-1 actor gives nothing measurable under any of the tried data, label or reference choices. The
round-2 data is not harmful (combining it with round 1 from SFT is level with round 1). A hypothesis, not tested
here: the limit is the round-1 actor's own distribution, whose successful episodes are longer and whose candidates
may be less diverse, leaving fewer useful contrasts.

Untested ideas, in the order EXPERIMENT_LOG §6 lists them: gate intermediate checkpoints on a validation pass
and stop early; action-only DPO (`OBJECTIVE=dpo_action`, which ignores the reasoning tokens that drive length);
length-matched pairs or a length-normalised loss; more round-2 data (it is about half of round 1's).
