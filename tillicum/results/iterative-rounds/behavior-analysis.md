# Why pairs from Round 1's candidates hurt: behaviour analysis (2026-11-19)

Models compared on one Online-Mind2Web pass (`dv-a`, 300 tasks, T 1.0, 30-step limit), all trained from SFT with
Round 1's RPO recipe:

- **data-matched single round** (`r2-sol2-ctrl`): half A + half B, with half B's candidates generated by **SFT**.
- **pooled** (`r2-dan-pooled@723`): half A + half B shuffled, with half B's candidates generated by **Round 1**.
- **seqsched** (`r2-dan-seqsched@723`) and **seqsched@542**: two epochs of half A, then half B (Round 1's candidates),
  as one continuous schedule.

SFT and Round 1 serve as references. Scripts and raw outputs are in
`/gpfs/scrubbed/piotrt/owrl/eval/redo2/behavior/`; `cases.txt` has step-by-step traces of 18 tasks the data-matched
model solves and both bad models fail. Each analysis ran as a 2-CPU MIG job.

**Caveats.** There is one sample per task, so per-task outcomes are noisy. The phrase and URL checks are regex
heuristics. The links from training data to behaviour are correlational.

## Where the gap comes from

These counts cover tasks valid in both models' runs, compared against the data-matched model. Most lost tasks end at
the step limit, and the bad models stop far less often than the data-matched model.

| model | data-matched's wins it loses | ...of those at the 30-step limit | wins it gains | `done` correct when emitted | episodes ending in `done` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| pooled | 38 | 23 | 12 | — | — |
| seqsched | 41 | 34 | 13 | 74% | 35–42% |
| seqsched@542 | 46 | 34 | 19 | 66% | 35–42% |
| data-matched | — | — | — | 58% | 63% |

When the seqsched models do answer, they are right more often than the data-matched model. They lose by **not
stopping**, not by answering wrongly.

## Findings, ranked by contribution to the gap

### 1. They don't stop when stuck, and they loop (largest)

**In evaluation:**
- **Rate of `done` per step.** pooled drops to 0.3–0.9% of steps after step 20, and seqsched stays at 1–2.7% from step
  3 on. The data-matched model stays at 3–6%.
- **Last 10 steps of episodes that hit the step limit** (bad models vs data-matched):

  | behaviour | bad models | data-matched |
  |---|---|---|
  | action leaves the URL unchanged | 65–70% | 52% |
  | same action as the previous step | 14–20% | 9% |
  | "verify / confirm / double-check" language | 16–17% (seqsched) | 3% |

- **Reasoning sentences copied word for word from the previous turn:** 12.5–13% for the bad models, 5.1% for
  data-matched and 13.5% for SFT. On steps where the URL didn't change it is 18–19% vs 8%.
- **Example:** on the Chris Paul task, seqsched clicks `[33,949]` 26 times in a row, each time repeating "my previous
  attempt to click on 'Per Game' didn't navigate…".

**Likely origin in the training data:**
- **Give-up language** ("exhausted all reasonable approaches", "try one final approach") appears in **12.9%** of the
  chosen responses built from Round 1's candidates, vs **5.3%** for those built from SFT's.
- **Give-up language without stopping:** only **15.3%** of those Round 1 responses actually emit `done` (31.7% for the
  SFT-candidate data, 36% for half A). seqsched reproduces that ratio: P(done | give-up phrase) is 16.9%, vs 31.7% for
  data-matched.
- **Mentions of the done function:** "use the done function" appears in 7.5% of Round 1–candidate *rejected*
  responses vs 4.6% of chosen ones. In seqsched's outputs it nearly disappears (0.1%, vs 2.7% for data-matched).
- **Not explained by the teacher's preferences on action type:** among pairs that contrast finishing with continuing,
  the teacher picks `done` *more* often in the Round 1–candidate data (34.7% vs 22.9%). What carries over appears to be
  the "stuck but keep going" phrasing.
- **History copying is not more frequent in any training set** (about 7%, chosen ≈ rejected). The bad models seem
  simply not to acquire the data-matched model's reduction in looping. This is unproven.

### 2. pooled's malformed actions: broken JSON in `done` answers (about 8 of its 38 lost tasks)

- **What's broken:** 160 failed steps (3.6% of pooled's steps), 129 of them `done` calls.

  | defect | count |
  |---|---|
  | literal newlines inside the answer string (10 also lack `</tool_call>`) | 95 |
  | unescaped double quotes inside the answer | ~33 |
  | the schema typo `"arguments {}}` | 3 |
  | no `<tool_call>` block at all | 4 |

- **After an error:** the model resends nearly the same answer until the harness aborts the episode.
- **Origin.** The training data has no parser failures. However, Round 1's chosen `done` answers are longer (median
  473 vs 383 characters) and more often multi-line (36.7%, vs 25.5% and 14.8% in the SFT-candidate sets). pooled copied
  that style (51% multi-line, median 813 characters) but lost the escaping. seqsched is 31% multi-line and escapes
  correctly; why only pooled breaks is unexplained.
- **Cheap mitigation:** an escape-repair fallback in the action parser would recover these episodes.

### 3. Navigation and scrolling habits carried over from Round 1's candidates (moderate, mostly inside the loops)

- **Large scrolls** (amount 0.8–1.0): 16.8% of Round 1–candidate chosen scrolls vs 3.5% of SFT-candidate ones;
  seqsched 34% vs data-matched 11%. seqsched makes 2.7 scrolls per episode vs 1.67.
- **Constructed URLs:**
  - **In the training data:** `goto_url` appears in 16.5% of Round 1–candidate chosen responses vs 10.2%, and 42% of
    those targets are built search-engine URLs vs 22%.
  - **In evaluation:** 34% of seqsched's `goto_url` targets are search URLs (18% for data-matched), and 41% are guessed
    deep links on other sites (30%). 21% of its search-engine visits hit a block page (14%).
  - **Examples:** `paullchr01.html` returned a 404; on the gluten-free task it opened 8 tabs in a burst.
- **Tabs:** the bad models open *fewer* new tabs than data-matched (0.9–1.1 per episode vs 1.34).

### 4. Weaker contrast between chosen and rejected in Round 1–candidate pairs (minor)

| | Round 1–candidate pairs | SFT-candidate pairs |
|---|---|---|
| near-identical pairs | 7.3% | 6.1% |
| chosen and rejected have the same action type | 22% | 14.5% |
| median shared reasoning prefix | 77 characters | 47 characters |

### Ruled out

- Malformed Round 1–candidate text (it has none).
- A shift in the overall action mix (within ±2 points of data-matched).
- Longer reasoning in the bad models.
- Mismatch between the stated plan and the executed action (4.5% vs 5.5%).
- Tab proliferation in aggregate.

## Representative cases (full traces in `cases.txt`)

- **XRP chart:** pooled makes the same first two clicks as data-matched, rejects "All" as not yearly, then loops on two
  clicks.
- **Chris Paul:** a guessed URL, then 26 identical clicks.
- **Albion Basin:** the bad models see "Not Released", which is the correct answer, but never report it.
- **Pharmacy job:** pooled hits blocked sites, then the episode is aborted after three malformed multi-line `done`
  answers.
- **Gluten-free diet:** seqsched opens a new tab with a constructed search URL on every step and hits the step limit.

## Suggested tests

- **Main hypothesis:** drop or rewrite the roughly 11% of Round 1–candidate pairs whose chosen response gives up
  without calling `done`, then retrain seqsched or pooled.
- **Structural fix:** regenerate half B's candidates with the student at Round 1's states (on-policy text), as
  discussed in the iterative README.
